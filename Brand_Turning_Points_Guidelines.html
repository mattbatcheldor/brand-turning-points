The identity and editorial handbook

Keep the work in view.

A field guide for designing, sourcing and publishing an archive that celebrates the creative decisions behind great campaigns.

01 / Editorial position

A film programme meets an archive.

The promise

Show the work, explain what changed and put the evidence where the reader can check it. Write for a curious marketer who wants to see the original and understand a decision they could use.

The voice

Use British English, short paragraphs and tangible detail. Open on the memorable moment: the hammer, the banknote, the two portraits. Credit the people and distinguish the documented result from our reading of the work.

Art direction

Use the wordmark in text. Use ink for the screening frame, paper for reading and lime for a primary action. Let a featured campaign bring its own accent palette into the artwork and film cards.

Use one strong cover image with a visible credit or an editorial illustration label. Keep grids aligned and case entries numbered. At narrow widths, stack the case narrative above its evidence and allow titles to wrap.

  1. Identify the case. Record brand, title, launch year, market, format and relevant creative credits. Say where the work was made if it differs from where it ran.
  2. Tell the story. Set up the problem, explain the creative decision and describe how the campaign invited people to see the brand differently. Give memorability its own paragraph grounded in a specific detail.
  3. State the impact. Label commercial, perception, behaviour, reach or creative legacy. Put the primary source beside the result and preserve its market, time period, denominator and attribution limits.
  4. Show the work and add a lesson. Link to the original artefact, identify its host and propose a credible way to test the idea today. State when campaign ROI or customer acquisition cost is unavailable.

04 / Moving image and provenance

Make it easy to watch and check.

Films and other artefacts

Prefer the original from the brand, agency, production company or a recognised archive. Include the title, uploader or collection, and any useful version details. Give print, packaging and outdoor work a link to the physical artefact or its catalogue entry.

Use a click-to-load YouTube player where the original allows it. Keep a visible watch link for blocked embeds and downloads. Use captions where available, leave sound off until the viewer starts playback and make the controls usable by keyboard.

YouTube Help · Embed videos and playlists

Evidence and stewardship

Prefer primary reporting, effectiveness papers and documented archival material. Attribute company or agency figures to their source. Give disputed figures their own scope and cite each source separately.

Label restorations, reconstructions and AI-generated illustrations clearly. Preserve the distinction between historical material and new artwork. Check that source links and playback work before publishing each edition.

05 / Release checklist

Before an edition goes live.

  • Open each case on a small screen and check the hierarchy, text contrast and keyboard focus.
  • Verify that every original-creative link leads to the credited work and every impact link supports the adjacent claim.
  • Check that each selected video opens, that the embedded player closes cleanly and that the external link remains visible.
  • Confirm that the case explains why the work is memorable, and that the evidence panel describes what the metric can support.
